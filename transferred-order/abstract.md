Order-invariant cluster first-order logic is included in first-order logic on bounded-degree graphs. A cluster first-order sentence is order-invariant if, on every finite graph over the given vocabulary, any two linear orders on its vertices give the sentence the same truth value. For every such sentence and every degree bound $d$, there is a first-order sentence over adjacency and the original unary predicates that agrees with it on all finite graphs of maximum degree at most $d$.

For each context depth $k$, we construct an order on a graph $A$ and transfer it to a graph $B$ that agrees with $A$ on first-order sentences up to a sufficiently large quantifier rank. On $A$, vertices of rare neighbourhood types initially form singleton blocks. We group the other vertices by their unary predicates and an auxiliary colour. At stage $i$ we choose the first and last vertices of each block, set apart the vertices close to those endpoints or to previously exceptional vertices, and split the remainder of each block by outer $i$-context. Any order extending the final sequence of blocks realises its set of context labels.

At depth $k$, put $r=k(k+1)/2$. Vertices are rare when their uncoloured radius-$r$ type occurs fewer than $t$ times. A finite threshold argument lets us choose $t$ large enough, relative to the number of rare vertices, to prescribe colours near the exceptional vertices and still realise every injective colouring of each frequent type elsewhere. The threshold and an upper bound on the number of exceptional vertices are determined from counts truncated at a uniform cap. We choose the first-order similarity rank to cover the count assertions and a sentence naming the full radius-$3r$ neighbourhood of the final exceptional set and identifying all rare vertices. We copy the auxiliary colours on its inner part and extend them to $B$. The images of the chosen endpoints determine the boundaries of the corresponding blocks. Their order and labels determine the sets of lower contexts between consecutive ball vertices and in the two outer intervals. The resulting orders realise the same outer $k$-contexts, so they agree on cluster sentences of quantifier rank at most $k+1$. Order invariance and first-order definability of the finitely many bounded-rank equivalence classes give the inclusion.
